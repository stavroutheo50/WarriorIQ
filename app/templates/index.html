Accuracy

How accurate it is, measured.

These are the checks done so far, on small sets of footage. They are findings, not validated accuracy figures, and they are why counts are labelled as estimates.

Flagged actions

On 178 clips from three reference fights, checked by hand, about a third of the actions the report displayed were real strikes.

One labelled bout

On a Kick Light bout labelled by a competitor, 11 of 16 counted moments were real strikes, and 6 of those had the right type.

Against official stats

On three UFC rounds, strikes thrown while both fighters stood were close to the official numbers (29 vs 21, 36 vs 12, 20 vs 17). In grappling the counts ran three to six times too high.

Guard

Not yet checked against hand-labelled clips, so it is marked experimental wherever it appears.

Questions

Before you upload

Is WarriorIQ an official judge?

No. {% if features.scoring.on %}Scorecards are evidence-gated estimates for training and review, not replacements for licensed officials.{% else %}WarriorIQ does not score fights yet; when score estimates arrive they will be training estimates, not a replacement for licensed officials.{% endif %}

Can I analyse a fight without an account?

No. A fight belongs to a workspace — your footage, the athletes in it and your upload allowance are all attached to an account — so WarriorIQ asks you to create one first. It is free, and your fights stay private to you.

How accurate are the strike counts?

Not accurate enough to treat as exact. They are labelled as rough estimates everywhere they appear; the accuracy checks above are why.

Can I see a report before signing up?

Yes. The sample report shows the layout with made-up fighters and numbers.

What happens when the AI is uncertain?

It leaves out anything the video cannot support and says so on the report. When it cannot tell the two fighters apart, the report asks you to point them out again on a clear picture.
