Accuracy

How accurate it is, measured.

These are the checks done so far, on small sets of footage. They are findings, not validated accuracy figures, and they are why counts are labelled as estimates.

Flagged actions

On 178 clips from three reference fights, checked by hand, about a third of the actions the report displayed were real strikes.

One labelled bout

On a Kick Light bout labelled by a competitor, 11 of 16 counted moments were real strikes, and 6 of those had the right type.

Counted by hand, 9 clips

A person marked every strike on 9 short clips (about 8 minutes of fighting, 111 strikes). Each fighter's total was typically within 2 strikes of the hand count, and the busier fighter was right on 6 of the 8 clips where the two differed. But only 38% of the individual counted moments matched a strike the person marked. That is why reports show totals and do not list individual strikes.

Time on the ground (MMA)

On five bouts labelled second by second, each judged by a model fitted on the other four, the ground-time check was right on 646 of 674 seconds, and every bout's ground time was within 10% of its labels. Who is on top is not measured.

Against official stats

On three UFC rounds, strikes thrown while both fighters stood ranged from near the official numbers to about three times them (29 vs 21, 36 vs 12, 20 vs 17). In grappling the counts ran three to six times too high.

Guard

Not yet checked against hand-labelled clips, so it is marked experimental wherever it appears.

Strike totals are labelled as estimates on every report for this reason. Movement, centre, pressure and balance come from body position and are not affected; guard stays marked experimental until it is checked. These are small sets of footage, labelled by us: findings, not independent validation.
